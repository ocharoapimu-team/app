Situation overview

A very strong El Niño has been under way since NOAA declared an El Niño Advisory on 11 June 2026 and is still strengthening. The Climate Prediction Center gives a very strong event a 100 per cent chance in September–November and October–December, an 83 per cent chance of a historic event (three-month RONI ≥ +2.5 °C) in October–December and a strong-or-stronger event more than 83 per cent likely through January–March 2027; ENSO-neutral becomes the most likely state only in April–June 2027. WMO forecasts an October–December Niño-3.4 of about +3.7 °C peaking in December, the highest since observations began, and BoM's weekly relative Niño3.4 set a record +2.81 °C in the week to 11 October, while monthly values remain below the long-term record.

It lands on a record-warm baseline — September 2026 was the warmest September on record (+1.02 °C against 1961–1990) after an all-time global sea-surface-temperature high in August — and on a region already absorbing the Middle East fuel-and-fertiliser shock. WMO holds that the event is not caused by climate change; attribution studies disagree; both sides agree that warming amplifies its impacts.

The dry side of the signal covers the Maritime Continent, the Philippines, Papua New Guinea and the western and southern Pacific; the wet side covers the central and eastern equatorial Pacific and Afghanistan. Drought has moved from forecast to declaration: Tonga on all its major islands, Papua New Guinea with the National Capital District at drought critical and 14 provinces at alert and 1.2 million people needing direct food relief, 106 Philippine areas under a state of calamity with PHP 5.56 billion in farm losses, and a drought emergency in West Nusa Tenggara as Indonesia's rainy season runs late over 61 per cent of its land. The most acute observed impact is the region's worst transboundary haze in 11 years: a Sarawak emergency at API 519, around 2 million Malaysian pupils out of school, Singapore's PSI at 192 and Timor-Leste's schools closed nationwide, with the haze now on the agenda of the November ASEAN summit.

In South Asia the monsoon failed — India closed at −12.6 per cent, with the East and Northeast at their driest since 1901 — and the risk has shifted to the rabi season and 2027, with Pakistan's peak need expected in January–March 2027. Prices compound the hazard: diesel is up 36.7 per cent worldwide and 89.9 per cent in Myanmar since February, August food inflation exceeded 8 per cent in Fiji, Kyrgyzstan, Sri Lanka and Pakistan, and WFP projects 49 million more people acutely food-insecure by the end of 2027.

Financing is thinner than the headlines suggest. CERF's US$100 million is a planning envelope; US$74 million across 13 El Niño-relevant frameworks is what is pre-arranged, and none of Asia-Pacific's three frameworks covers drought. The FAO–WFP joint appeal seeks US$202 million for 8.8 million people, IOM US$110 million, and food-sector humanitarian aid is 59 per cent below its 2022 peak. The sharpest agricultural impacts are expected from November 2026, and the decay year, 2027, is judged the period of highest concern — strength is not impact, and impact often lands in the year after the peak.

    The Indian Ocean Dipole is disputed

    BoM's IOD index has run at +0.88 °C for five weeks without a positive IOD being declared (11 Oct); the SASCOF Oct–Dec consensus reads the IOD as neutral with the Indian Ocean warm basin-wide (14 Oct); WMO had a positive IOD peaking in October at an OND mean of ~+0.5 °C (6 Oct), after forecasting a SON mean of +0.9 °C (1 Sep). For South Asia the IOD often matters more than El Niño alone. Detail: south-asian-monsoon-drivers.

    Prices — three shocks compounding

    WFP Asia-Pacific, Spillover Pathways #8 · 5 Oct 2026

    The Strait of Hormuz disruption, renewed attacks at Bab al-Mandeb and the Black Sea wheat corridor (27 per cent of world wheat exports) now stack on El Niño. The fuel round came first; the food round is following. Bloomberg puts El Niño's own CPI impact at +1.87 pp for Indonesia and +1.85 pp for the Philippines, which Allianz disputes; the long-run cost of the event is put at more than US$4 trillion through 2030.

    What governments did about fuel (ESCAP/WFP count of policy actions)

    • 30 economies — fuel subsidies and tax cuts
    • 28 — price controls (caps, staggered hikes)
    • 20 — demand reduction (conservation, remote work, shorter school days)
    • 16 — strategic-reserve adjustments and export restrictions
    • 8 — targeted assistance and demand prioritisation

    Supply tightening on the food side: FAO sees global rice output −1.6% and India −6.5%, the largest fall in two decades, with Thailand −2%. Pump prices: Myanmar diesel +89.9%, Indonesia +85.4%, world average +36.7%. ADB cut Pacific growth to 3.0% for 2026 (from 3.3%) "because the El Niño is now expected to be more intense".
